“Building Stories” Takes Readers on an Imaginative Tour Through the World of Children Books

On Sunday, January 21, 2024, the National Building Museum in Washington, D.C., unveiled its landmark new exhibition, “Building Stories.” This immersive and multigenerational experience invites visitors to explore the fascinating intersection of architecture, engineering, construction, and design as depicted in children’s literature.On Sunday, January 21, 2024, the National Building Museum in Washington, D.C., unveiled its landmark new exhibition, “Building Stories.” This immersive and multigenerational experience invites visitors to explore the fascinating intersection of architecture, engineering, construction, and design as depicted in children’s literature.

What Is “Building Stories”?

  • Exhibition Overview: Building Stories occupies 4,000 square feet of prime exhibition space on the Museum’s ground floor. It’s the most ambitious exhibition the Museum has ever undertaken, and it will remain on display for an impressive ten years.
  • Curator and Design: The exhibition was curated by Leonard Marcus, a leading expert in children’s literature. Collaborating with Portland-based design studio Plus And Greater Than, Building Stories brings the wonder of the built environment to life through the lens of beloved children’s books.

Exploring the Galleries

Gallery One: “Building Readers”
This introductory gallery delves into a child’s early experiences with shapes, forms, imagery, and words—the building blocks of language and the built environment.
Visitors discover parallels between book design and architectural construction, exploring rare book dummies, original sketches, and architectural models.

Gallery Two: “Your Home, My Home”
Inspired by the Three Little Pigs, this gallery features three archways that connect to immersive round theaters.
Multimedia presentations bring iconic books like Tar Beach, Shadow, and The Snowy Day to life, emphasizing the concept of “home” in various forms—whether it’s a bedroom, a house, or an entire neighborhood.

Gallery Three: “Scale Play”
Visitors enter through a magical threshold, feeling as though they’re changing size.
This gallery challenges perceptions of the world through “scale play,” a recurring theme in children’s literature.

The Heartfelt Design Lessons

Building Stories highlights how children’s books influence our early perceptions of home and the built environment. Spaces become characters in these stories, inviting us to see the world anew.
